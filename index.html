Background

About Hon. Justina Jonas

SWAPO Member of Parliament. Chairperson, Standing Committee on Poverty Eradication, Labour & Industrial Relations. Trade unionist. Champion of workers' rights.

SWAPO Party · 8th National Assembly (2025–2030)

Hon. Justina Jonas, MP

Hon. Justina Jonas is a Member of Parliament of the Republic of Namibia and a long-serving trade unionist with more than two decades of experience in the labour movement. She serves in the 8th National Assembly as a representative of the SWAPO Party and is the Chairperson of the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Poverty Eradication, Labour and Industrial Relations.

Born on 10 February 1978, Hon. Jonas grew up in Ohadiwa in northern Namibia. Her early life was shaped by rural life and responsibility, experiences that contributed to her commitment to working people and social justice. Before entering the labour movement, she worked in different occupations, including as a security guard, kapana vendor and hairdresser.

Her trade union journey began in the construction sector, where she became a member of the Metal and Allied Namibian Workers Union (MANWU). In 2003, she moved to Windhoek and joined MANWU, initially volunteering before becoming the union's first receptionist. She subsequently served as a branch organiser and became involved in worker education and labour organising.

Through dedication, worker education and organisational leadership, Hon. Jonas rose through the ranks of MANWU and was elected its first woman General Secretary in 2012. She served in that position for many years, representing workers in collective bargaining, labour relations, occupational health and safety, social protection, worker education and other matters affecting working people. She was again elected General Secretary at MANWU's 10th National Congress in 2024.

Her transition from trade union leadership to Parliament in 2025 marked a continuation of her public-service commitment. In her maiden parliamentary statement, she described herself as coming from the workers' movement and emphasised her responsibility to bring workers' concerns and experiences into the National Assembly.

As a Parliamentarian, Hon. Jonas has focused particularly on labour rights, employment creation, social protection, poverty eradication, decent work, occupational health and safety, industrial relations and inclusive national development. Her parliamentary work includes oversight of policies and programmes affecting workers, vulnerable communities and Namibia's social protection system.

Hon. Jonas is studying a Bachelor of Business Administration at STADIO Namibia and she holds a Higher Certificate in Management, a Diploma in Labour Studies and a Certificate in Political Science. She has also undertaken training in labour studies, social security protection, corporate governance, occupational health and safety, social dialogue and worker education.

Her public-service journey reflects a progression from the workplace and trade union movement into national leadership. She continues to advocate for the dignity of work, protection of workers' rights, social justice and inclusive economic development, while contributing to Namibia's legislative and parliamentary oversight processes.

“From the workplace to Parliament, my mission remains to serve, represent and empower working people and all Namibians.”

Recent Parliamentary Statements

Full text of statements delivered in the National Assembly by Hon. Justina Jonas, MP (SWAPO-Party).

Vote 13 · Health & Social Welfare

Statement on Vote 13: Health and Social Welfare

Read the statement ▾

Chairperson of the Whole House,

I rise in support of Vote 13, with particular focus on the programme concerning Public Works and Public Property.

The Ministry has been allocated N$780 million for infrastructure development under the National Development Budget, as reflected on page 84 of the document. This allocation is vital to ensuring that the Ministry delivers quality healthcare services, enhances access to public health facilities, and promotes the overall well-being of society.

Infrastructure development does more than just improve facilities; it is a key engine for job creation, both directly through employment in construction, maintenance, and associated services, and indirectly, by stimulating related sectors such as retail, logistics, and hospitality. I am confident that this N$780 million allocation will contribute significantly to boosting economic activity and employment during this financial year.

However, Honourable Minister, as a proud labour unionist and advocate for decent work, I must raise an important concern. The construction sector, despite its potential for job creation, is often plagued by non-compliance with labour laws and exploitative working conditions.

In June 2024, a collective agreement was gazetted between the Metal and Allied Namibian Workers Union (MANWU) and the Construction Industries Federation (CIF). This agreement governs the minimum conditions of employment, including wages, hours, benefits, and safety standards for all construction workers employed in the sector.

Regrettably, many contractors who are awarded government-funded projects, including those under the Ministry of Health and Social Welfare, have failed to comply with this gazetted agreement. This non-compliance directly undermines workers’ rights, distorts fair competition among contractors, and contradicts the government’s commitment to decent work.

Therefore, Honourable Minister, I propose without any additional monetary implication that your Ministry, as the client and project supervisor, strengthens compliance monitoring mechanisms. Specifically:

  • During monthly site inspections, Ministry officials should include a compliance checklist aligned with the gazetted collective agreement.
  • Officials must verify whether workers are paid in line with gazetted rates, provided with personal protective equipment (PPE), and working under legally acceptable conditions.
  • Non-compliant contractors should be flagged, and their conduct reported to the Ministry of Justice and Labour Relations for enforcement action.

Honourable Chairperson,

If we are serious about using infrastructure investment to build a better Namibia, we must ensure that the very workers building these facilities are treated with dignity, fairness, and respect.

Let us remember: Decent work is not a favour; it is a right.

In conclusion, Honourable Minister, are you aware of the 97 former health extension workers from Okongo district who worked the whole year of 2014 without pay? It is my understanding that the Ministry has refused to pay these 97 workers despite paying their fellow colleagues for the same case from Omusati in 2018/2019. It is my understanding that NAPWU is directed to submit this case at the Office of the Labour Commission — is this really necessary rather than the Ministry meeting with NAPWU and these employees to resolve the matter?

With that, I fully support Vote 13 and look forward to seeing these projects deliver both improved healthcare infrastructure and fair, dignified employment.

I thank you.

Vote 23 & 24 · Works and Transport

Statement on Vote 23 & 24: Works and Transport

Read the statement ▾

Chairperson of the Whole House,

I rise in full support of Vote 23 and 24, under the programme of Construction and Building Works.

Honourable Minister, allow me to begin by commending your leadership and tireless energy since assuming office. Your commitment and visibility have been refreshing, and we pray that the same momentum is cascaded throughout the entire Ministry. It is our sincere hope that under your guidance, the Ministry will serve as a beacon of hope and revival for the construction sector, which currently employs 28,661 workers, as reported in the 2023 Labour Force Survey.

I also wish to congratulate you on the Ministry’s efforts to promote the inclusion of local contractors in public works. For too long, concerns have been raised about the exclusion of Namibian contractors from major infrastructure projects, such as the Karibib–Usakos road, funded by KfW, and the Hosea Kutako Airport Dual Carriageway (Phases 1, 2, and 3), co-financed by the African Development Bank, China-Aid, and the Government of Namibia.

The prevailing narrative that our local contractors lack capacity or fail to meet excessive financial requirements is both misleading and detrimental. If we are to develop and empower a competitive domestic construction industry, we must intentionally build and grow local capacity. This includes identifying capable contractors and supporting them through targeted interventions so they can scale from national to multinational operations. The time to act is now.

Another critical matter is the delay in the establishment of the National Construction Council. This Council is essential to:

  • Promote fair competition,
  • Prioritise Namibian contractors in national infrastructure development,
  • Regulate and categorise contractors,
  • Evaluate bids for government tenders more effectively, and
  • Curb corrupt practices in the tendering process.

Honourable Minister, I respectfully ask: what is the status of the National Construction Council Bill?

However, I must reiterate a concern previously raised under other votes: there is a need for strengthened monitoring and enforcement of labour standards and rights on all capital projects funded or overseen by government entities. The Ministry must ensure that contractors, both local and foreign, comply with fair labour practices, provide safe working conditions, and uphold the dignity and rights of every worker involved.

Value Addition: Workers’ Rights and Development

As we deliberate these votes, let us remember that behind every kilometre of road and every brick laid in public buildings are thousands of Namibian workers, men and women whose sweat and skill drive our national development.

Furthermore, I wish to emphasise the importance of integrating vocational training graduates into public infrastructure projects. Our Vocational Training Centres continue to produce skilled artisans, many of whom struggle to find gainful employment. I urge the Ministry to form partnerships with VTCs across the country and ensure that government maintenance contracts prioritise these young artisans. Doing so will not only empower the youth but also provide hands-on experience and promote local economic development.

Transport

Honourable Minister, allow me to also commend you and your team for the bold decision to regularise the operations of seven-seater taxis, thereby creating a path for thousands of operators to earn a decent living. Your Ministry, together with its agencies and the hardworking construction workforce, has helped Namibia retain its standing as one of the African countries with the best road infrastructure.

Maiden Speech 9 April 2025

Maiden Speech: 2025/26 National Budget Debate

In her inaugural parliamentary address, Hon. Jonas called on government to finalise modernisation of the Labour Act, the Social Security Act, and the Affirmative Action (Employment) Act. She championed enforcement of the national minimum wage across vulnerable sectors — charcoal, retail, logistics, security and domestic workers — and supported the budget's 500,000 new jobs target over five years. She declared: 'I am a deployee of the workers.'

Committee Work 2025

Social Protection Parliamentary Oversight Workshop — Swakopmund

As Committee Chairperson, Hon. Jonas led a national parliamentary workshop on social protection in Swakopmund. She highlighted that over 618,000 Namibians rely on state grants — including 202,294 old-age pensioners and 356,756 children on OVC grants — and called for urgent strengthening of the social safety net, stating: 'We have all taken an oath to serve with integrity and to strive for a dignified life for every citizen.'

Debate September 2025

Fixed-Term Contracts & Exploitation in the Workplace

Hon. Jonas tabled a motion on the exploitative use of short-term employment contracts, exposing a systematic pattern where multinational corporations retrench permanent employees and rehire them through subcontractors at lower wages with no pensions. She described this as directly undermining government's decent work agenda and called for urgent legislative enforcement to protect Namibian workers from exploitation.

Manufacturing Sector Investment Advocacy

Hon. Jonas advocated urgently for investment in Namibia's manufacturing sector, warning that without a robust manufacturing base, Namibia continues to export raw materials and outsource jobs — creating dangerous dependency on foreign markets. She argued that manufacturing is the primary pathway to sustainable, decent employment and must be treated as a national development priority.

Major Report February 2026

Mining Sector Labour Audit — 11 Mines Investigated Across 5 Regions

In a landmark committee report, Hon. Jonas presented findings from oversight visits to 11 mines across five regions. The committee found systematic abuse: permanent employees retrenched and rehired through subcontractors with inferior wages, no pensions, and no job security. She exposed the misuse of 'voluntary separation packages' to circumvent Section 34 of the Labour Act and called for immediate legislative enforcement across all mining corporations.
